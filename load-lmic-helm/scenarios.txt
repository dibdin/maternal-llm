Who the scenario is about
242 (76%) center on a maternal patient, 58 (18%) on a neonate or newborn, and 14 (4%) are told from the perspective of a health worker or system actor.
Clinical domains (multi-label, so totals exceed 318)
The dataset is heavily weighted toward neonatal prematurity/LBW (68 scenarios, 21%) and HIV/ART/PMTCT (46, 14%) — reflecting how dominant these are in Bangladesh and Zimbabwe epidemiology. The next tier: anemia/nutrition (32, 10%), hypertension/eclampsia (29, 9%), hemorrhage (25, 8%), obstructed labor and adolescent/child marriage (19 each, 6%). Birth asphyxia (17), neonatal sepsis (13), twins (14), stillbirth (12), maternal sepsis (12), and social/structural barriers (12) round out the mid-tier. Where it takes place
Home birth or home setting is the most common single context at 59 scenarios (19%), followed by community health post/CHW visits (46, 14%) and district hospital (34, 11%). Rural/remote settings appear in 21 (7%), emergency units in 12 (4%). Only 3 scenarios are set at private clinics. The dataset is strongly oriented toward out-of-facility and low-resource care.
Who is providing care
A physician or clinician is present or consulted in 95 scenarios (30%), a nurse in 54 (17%), a CHW in 27 (8%), and a midwife in 22 (7%). Traditional birth attendants appear in 12 scenarios (4%), doulas in 4, and spiritual birth attendants in 2 — covering the full informal care provider spectrum present in LMIC settings.
Maternal age (where specified, n = 253)
47% of maternal patients are aged 25–34, 34% are 18–24, 11% are adolescents (≤17), and 8% are ≥35. The adolescent representation is intentionally elevated given the high child marriage rates in Bangladesh and Zimbabwe.
